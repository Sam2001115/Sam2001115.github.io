PROJECT:
CNC_PLOTTER

✓ COMPLETED MECHATRONICS EMBEDDED G-CODE

01. Objective

Design and build a functional 2-axis CNC plotter machine capable of drawing vector graphics on paper. The machine was salvaged from decommissioned inkjet printer parts — reusing the stepper motors, guide rails, and belt drive mechanisms to minimise cost while achieving accurate pen positioning.

The project required custom firmware, G-code parsing, and a motor control interface, alongside a custom UI built on the ESP32's onboard display.

03. How It Works

Frame & Motion: The X-axis uses a salvaged printer carriage rail and belt driven by one stepper. The Y-axis moves the paper bed using the second stepper from the same printer. This keeps the build cost near zero for mechanical parts while maintaining genuine linear motion accuracy.

Pen Control: A small servo attached to the pen mount lifts and lowers the pen (Z-axis), allowing the plotter to move between strokes without marking the paper.

G-Code Parsing: The ESP32 firmware reads G-code commands over serial (G0/G1 moves, M3/M5 pen up/down). Each command is parsed and translated into stepper pulses via the A4988 drivers with configurable steps/mm.

// G-code execution loop (simplified) G0 X__ Y__ Rapid move → pen up, fast traverse G1 X__ Y__ Draw move → pen down, controlled feed rate M3 Pen DOWN → servo lowers pen to paper M5 Pen UP → servo raises pen (travel)

Display UI: The built-in ST7789 TFT shows a CNC machine background image with a live status overlay — displaying current coordinates, job progress, and feed rate. This was implemented using custom sprite rendering on the ESP32.

04. Results

✅ Successfully plotted vector designs including geometric patterns and text at approximately ±1mm accuracy.

✅ Full G-code compatibility with standard CAM tools (Inkscape G-code extension).

✅ Custom TFT UI operational — live position and status display during plotting.

✅ Build cost kept minimal by salvaging all mechanical components from discarded printers.

05. Lessons Learned

Salvaged Parts Require Reverse Engineering

Printer steppers have no published datasheet for that specific unit — coil resistance measurement and trial step voltages were needed to identify safe operating current limits for the A4988 drivers.

Steps-Per-MM Calibration Is Critical

Incorrect microstepping or belt pitch assumptions cause scaling errors in every plot. Calibrating with a known-dimension test square before any real job is essential.

Pen Pressure Matters

Too much servo force on Z caused paper distortion; too little caused skipped strokes. A spring-loaded pen mount was added to absorb surface variation and maintain consistent contact force.
