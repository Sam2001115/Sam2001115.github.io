PROJECT:
CLEANING_ROVER

✓ OPERATIONAL EMBEDDED SYSTEMS ROBOTICS IoT CAD / 3D PRINT

01. Objective

Design and build a fully autonomous cleaning rover capable of navigating a room unattended — scanning its surroundings, avoiding obstacles, and driving a brush mechanism. The rover also needed a WiFi control interface for manual override, speed adjustment, and live sensor monitoring from any phone or laptop without requiring a router.

03. The Challenges

Challenge 1 — Motor Torque: The 130 DC motor was initially chosen for its small size and high RPM. Under real chassis load, however, the torque was far too low to move the robot.

Challenge 2 — Motor Driver Voltage Drop: To compensate, a 12T:60T gear reduction (5:1 ratio) was 3D-printed and the L298N driver was used. The moment the wheels contacted the ground and motors drew real current, the L298N caused a severe voltage drop that threatened the entire circuit.

Challenge 3 — Logic Level Mismatch: The ESP32 GPIO operates at 3.3V, but the HC-SR04 Echo pin outputs 5V. Connecting directly would permanently damage the ESP32's internal registers.

Challenge 4 — Chassis Redesign Under Pressure: Switching to TT motors required a full chassis redesign in SolidWorks. One reprinted part was deformed during assembly by a heat gun. The RMIT 3D printing room was also closed at the time, requiring a trip to a public makerspace to complete the prints.

Challenge 5 — Power Rail Noise: Four motors, four ultrasonic sensors, and an ESP32 on a shared battery required careful rail isolation to prevent motor switching noise from crashing the microcontroller.

04. The Solutions

Motor & Driver — Two Build Iterations:

✗ Iteration 1 — Failed

130 DC motors + 3D-printed 12T:60T gear reduction (5:1)

L298N motor driver

Outcome: Severe voltage drop under load — L298N inefficiency threatened circuit damage. Scrapped.

✓ Iteration 2 — Final Build

TT Gear Motors (6V, built-in gearbox, wheels included)

DRV8833 — efficient, low dropout, no thermal issues

Outcome: Stable torque, zero voltage collapse, 50+ hour runtime achieved.

DRV8833 Control Logic: Unlike the L298N, the DRV8833 has no separate enable pin. Speed is controlled by PWM directly on the IN pins. EEP pins are bridged to VCC on the modules for always-on operation.

// DRV8833 — No Enable Pin. PWM on IN pins directly. FORWARD IN1 = PWM_val | IN2 = LOW BACKWARD IN1 = LOW | IN2 = PWM_val STOP IN1 = LOW | IN2 = LOW EEP pins Bridged to VCC on module (always enabled)

Logic Level Fix: A two-resistor voltage divider per sensor steps the 5V Echo signal down to 3.3V before reaching the ESP32 GPIO. Four dividers cover all four sensors (front, back, left, right).

Power Architecture — Dual Isolated Rails:

// Power Architecture SOURCE 6× 18650 3S2P → 11.1V / 6000mAh [3S BMS Protected] RAIL A EY9 Regulator → 5V [ESP32 + All Sensors] RAIL B XL4015 Buck Conv → 6V [DRV8833 #1 Drive + DRV8833 #2 Brushes] SENSE A ACS712 → GPIO35 ADC [Current monitoring] SENSE B V-Divider R1=100kΩ R2=32.2kΩ → GPIO34 ADC [Battery voltage]

Brush System: The original 130 DC motors were repurposed as brush drivers, managed by a dedicated second DRV8833 integrated into the power schematic.

WiFi Interface — Access Point Mode: The ESP32 broadcasts its own WiFi network — no router required. Any device connects to CleaningRobot_AP and opens the control dashboard in a browser. Features:

Radar Obstacle Display Manual Drive Controls Auto Obstacle Avoidance Drive Motor Speed Slider Brush Motor Speed Slider Live Battery Voltage ACS712 Current Monitor

07. Lessons Learned

Torque Under Load Is the Real Spec

RPM ratings are misleading. The 130 DC motor failed immediately under chassis weight. Always validate torque at the actual operating load, not just the datasheet number.

Driver Efficiency Is Critical in Battery Systems

The L298N's voltage drop under load was severe enough to risk circuit damage. The DRV8833 eliminated the problem entirely — lower dropout, less heat, smaller board. For any battery-powered robot, driver efficiency is non-negotiable.

Always Verify Logic Levels Before Connecting

The 5V Echo signal would have permanently damaged the ESP32 GPIO registers. A two-resistor voltage divider costs almost nothing. Catching this in the planning phase saved the microcontroller.

Isolate Logic and Motor Power Rails

Motors generate noisy, spiky currents. Sharing a rail with the ESP32 causes brownouts and random resets. The EY9/XL4015 dual-rail design eliminated all instability.

PLA and Heat Don't Mix

A heat gun deformed a freshly printed chassis part during assembly. PLA softens around 60°C — keep all heat sources clear when working near printed parts, and allow adequate airflow around motor mounts.

Measure Before You Print

A small measurement error in the TT motor mount was only discovered after printing. The RMIT 3D printing room was also closed that week, making the reprint more painful. Measure twice, model once.
