PROJECT: CLEANING_ROVER

01. Objective

The goal of this project is to build a cleaning rover that would regularly clean the room it's placed in unattended.

02. Technology Used

ESP32 DevKit V1 DRV8833 Motor Driver TT Gear Motors HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensors 130 DC Motors (Brushes) 18650 Li-ion (11.1V) Buck Converters

03. The Challenges

The challenges faced in the process of building this project were finding the right motors and the motor driver chip to use to run them. After researching, the choice was the 130 DC motor for its small size with high RPM; however, there was a problem of torque, thus the 130 DC motor's torque is very low. Another challenge faced was the battery unit to use in this project. The final challenge was the use of ultrasonic sensors with the ESP32. The ESP32 receives a signal in 3.3V, but the ultrasonic sensors' echo pin sends a 5V signal; thus, connecting the Echo pin to the ESP32 directly would damage it.

04. The Solutions

The solution for the torque challenge was to 3D print a gear reduction mechanism containing a 12-tooth gear and a 60-tooth gear with a ratio of 5:1, and the motor drive chip chosen was the L298n. However, that caused a voltage drop as soon as the wheels touched the ground, as the motors draw current, and that would cause significant damage to the circuit.

The solution for this problem was achieved through using the TT motor with a built-in gear unit and replacing the L298n with a DRV8833, as it’s efficient and doesn't release as much heat as the L298n; thus, the torque and motor driver problem was solved. As well as a great addition was adding a wifi interface that let me control the rover manually as well as putting it on auto mode while also a window that shows how far obstacles are.

// Power Configuration
Source: 3x 18650E 3.7V (11.1V Total)
Logic Rail: Buck Converter to 5V (ESP32)
Motor Rail: Buck Converter to 6V (DRV8833)

As for the Ultrasonic Echo pin challenge, it was overcome by using a voltage divider of two resistors to lower the signal voltage to 3.3V so that the ESP32 can receive it safely by placing four of them to scan the robot's surroundings. For the brushes, the 130DC motors were placed there, as well as adding the DRV8833 to the schematics to run them with ordinary cleaning rover brushes attached to it.

05. Results

✅ Finally, the robot is operating perfectly, and the goal was achieved. The rover scans its surroundings perfectly, navigates around the room, and cleans the room effectively, running for around 50 Hours.
