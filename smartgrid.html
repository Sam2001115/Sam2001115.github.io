01. Objective

Model, simulate, and analyse a multi-bus power distribution network using DIgSILENT PowerFactory as part of the EEET2613 Smart Grids course. The project involved building a realistic network topology with generation, transmission, load, and renewable energy elements, then running steady-state load flow and contingency analyses to evaluate system stability and efficiency.

03. What Was Analysed

Steady-State Load Flow: Newton-Raphson load flow was used to solve bus voltages, line currents, and power flows across the network under normal operating conditions. Bus voltage profiles were checked against ±5% nominal limits.

N-1 Contingency: Each critical line and transformer was removed in turn to simulate a single-element outage. The simulation identified which contingencies caused overloads or voltage violations, and reactive compensation points were added to restore compliance.

Renewable Integration: A PV solar farm was added to a suitable bus. The load flow was re-run at different solar output levels (0%, 50%, 100%) to observe voltage rise and reverse power flow effects on the feeder.

05. Lessons Learned

Voltage Rise Is the Key Challenge for High PV Penetration

As solar generation increased, reverse power flow caused voltage rise beyond acceptable limits on the feeder. This is a real constraint on how much distributed solar a network can absorb without active voltage management.

N-1 Is the Minimum Reliability Standard

Utilities design to N-1 — any single element can fail without affecting customers. Running the full contingency matrix revealed unexpected weak points that weren't obvious from the base case load flow alone.

Reactive Power Location Matters

Adding reactive compensation at the wrong bus (high impedance path from the problem bus) had almost no effect. Placing it directly at the low-voltage bus gave immediate compliance — location is as important as magnitude.
