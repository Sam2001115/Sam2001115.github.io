01. Objective

Design a lunar surface exploration rover as the Assessment 2 group project for OENG1250 (Engineering Design 1). The brief required the team to produce a fully detailed mechanical design — 3D CAD model, assembly drawings, bill of materials, and design justification — suitable for prototype fabrication.

The rover had to satisfy defined mission requirements: traverse rough lunar terrain, carry a scientific payload, survive thermal extremes, and be deployable from a lander without astronaut intervention.

03. Design Overview

Chassis: A central monocoque aluminium chassis houses the battery pack, electronics bay, and payload compartment. Aluminium was selected for its high strength-to-weight ratio and known behaviour in vacuum and thermal cycling environments.

Suspension: A rocker-bogie suspension system — the same architecture used on NASA's Mars rovers — was designed to allow any two wheels to remain in contact with the surface while the rover traverses obstacles up to 1.5× wheel diameter. This was modelled as a full articulating assembly in SolidWorks.

Wheels: Six 200mm diameter wheels with radial cleats, designed for loose regolith (fine lunar soil). Hollow aluminium construction minimises mass while maintaining structural integrity under lunar gravity loading.

Thermal Management: Multi-layer insulation (MLI) blankets wrap the electronics bay. A passive heat rejection louver on the chassis top opens at high temperatures to radiate excess heat — no active cooling required within the design operating envelope.

Deployment: The rover folds into a compact stowed configuration for lander integration — fold-down wheel arms reduce the footprint by 60%. A spring-loaded deployment sequence releases all six arms simultaneously on a single command signal.

06. Lessons Learned

Group CAD Requires Strict File Management

Multiple people modelling in one SolidWorks assembly caused reference errors when parts were renamed or moved between sessions. Establishing a shared file naming convention and folder structure at the start would have saved significant rework time.

The Rocker-Bogie Geometry Has Specific Constraints

The rocker arm lengths and pivot positions aren't arbitrary — they're geometrically derived to ensure equal normal force distribution across all six wheels. Getting this wrong produces a mechanically sound-looking assembly that fails the terrain traversal requirement.

Mass Properties Drive Everything

Every design decision — wheel diameter, chassis dimensions, payload placement — feeds back into the mass properties and centre-of-mass position. Running this analysis early and iterating on it throughout the design process, rather than checking it at the end, would have avoided late-stage rework.
